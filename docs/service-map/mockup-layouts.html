Service map layouts

Four ways to lay out the same example system: an online shop with 14 services and 6 databases, queues and external APIs, scanned over the past 60 minutes. Step through the five checkpoints of a scan to see how each layout behaves as connections arrive. The rule we care about most is that nodes already on screen should not move. Orange dashed lines show nodes that moved at the current step. The data is made up but has realistic proportions.

The same centred-flow algorithm on systems from empty to about 480 nodes. In large systems, a column that would grow past a fixed height wraps into extra lanes. "Moves" counts nodes that were already on screen and changed position at a later step, with the total distance in layout pixels (about 150 px between rings or columns). Nodes joining from the "not connected yet" tray are not counted. The force layout caps each move at 40 px, which is why its numbers are small but never zero.

All 20 nodes are known on the first frame because the aggregate query lists every service and every database or external call (decision R2-6). What arrives page by page are the connections between services. Until a service connects to the rest of the map, it waits in the tray at the bottom.
